## 01 Context · Data Requirements

**Required sources, and exclusions**

Raw Multimodal Stream: Support tickets (Zendesk), call notes (Salesforce/HubSpot), interview transcripts (Gong/Otter), executive emails, and internal developer channels (Slack).
Metadata Normalization: Every ingested artifact is stamped with:
Source type & authority tier (User verbatim vs. Enterprise customer vs. Internal engineering vs. Executive).
Noise Discard Policy: Explicit heuristics to isolate signal from conversational filler:
Filtered out: Sarah’s barking dog, casual aesthetic comments ("blue bar hurts eyes", "dark mode"), unvetted sales pandering ("maybe TikTok integration").
Retained: The deterministic failure modes and hard contract blockers.

**How fresh must it be**

Weekly Rolling Window (Fixed Cadence):
Juno’s primary unit of time is a rolling 7-day window (e.g., previous Monday 00:00 UTC through Sunday 23:59 UTC).
Why near-real-time is explicitly avoided for the Pulse:
Real-time streaming creates recency bias and whipsaws product priorities. A 7-day batch window buffers against single-day spikes (like one frustrated user having a bad morning) while capturing persistent behavioral patterns and systemic breakdowns over the full operating week.

## 02 Tools · System Capabilities

**The verb list**

Ingested Modalities & Sources:
Qualitative text from five distinct sources: customer interview transcripts (Sarah), enterprise escalation tickets (Mike Ross / Pearson Co), executive email directives (Gavin Belson), CRM sales notes (Jessica L. / Acme Corp), and engineering Slack threads (dev_dave in #backend-monitoring).
Metadata & Authority Attribution:
Every ingested artifact is automatically tagged with its origin tier and business exposure:
End-User Operational Pain: Sarah (workflow loss, core feature failure).
High-ARR Churn Risk: Pearson Co ($50k/year contract, board meeting blocker).
Executive Strategy Mandate: Gavin Belson (CEO, Q3 competitor response).
Contractual Revenue Gate: Acme Corp (enterprise prospect, hard compliance requirement).
Technical Feasibility / Health: Dev Dave (infrastructure bottleneck, backend redline).
Explicit Noise-Discard Rules:
Aesthetic and casual remarks are filtered out: Sarah’s barking dog, complaints about the navigation bar being "too bright," and offhand requests for "dark mode" are logged to a secondary backlog but excluded from the executive pulse.
Unvalidated sales chatter is filtered out: Sales rep Jessica’s exploratory note about a "TikTok integration" is discarded as noise with zero strategic grounding.
Emotional hyperbole is separated from facts: Mike Ross’s ALL-CAPS screaming is normalized to extract the actual root issues: broken RBAC permissions on seat assignment (Error 403) and inaccessible invoicing.

**Deliberate omissions**

The Deliberately Omitted Tool: Direct Jira / Linear Ticket-Creation Tool
What it is:
The ability for Juno to automatically convert customer issues, synthesis themes, or technical bottlenecks into newly created backlog tickets, user stories, or bug reports in Jira or Linear.

Why it was withheld (The Product Decision):

Prevents Backlog Pollution:
Automated ticket creation quickly clutters engineering backlogs with hundreds of duplicate, low-conviction, or poorly scoped tickets. If Juno could write to Jira, Sarah’s offhand remark about dark mode and Jessica’s vague question about a TikTok integration would become formal tickets that engineers have to spend time grooming and closing.
Preserves the PM’s Role as Gatekeeper:
Deciding what gets built and when is a core human judgment call involving product vision, company strategy, and resource trade-offs. If an AI automatically tickets every problem it detects, it blurs the line between observing reality and committing roadmaps.
Protects Strategic Context:
Looking at Raw_Signals.md, a naive agent tool would create a ticket saying "Build AI email generator per CEO email." In reality, that would destabilize the database. The PM needs to read Dev Dave's infra warning before any Jira ticket is created. Juno’s job is to arm the PM with the evidence to make that call—not to create the ticket itself.
Secondary Omitted Tool: Customer-Facing Auto-Reply / Ticket-Resolution Tool
Why it was withheld:
Juno is strictly an internal analytical filter. Giving it write-access to Zendesk or email to reply to customers (e.g., trying to soothe Mike Ross during his 403 lockout) risks making unapproved promises, triggering PR fallout, or falsely closing unresolved enterprise escalations.

## 03 Loop · AI Costs & Latency

**Turn ceiling and escalation**

Strict Quote & Artifact Grounding (Zero Speculation):
Every theme, sentiment rating, and recommendation must cite at least one verbatim excerpt from the ingested artifacts.
The agent is forbidden from attributing motivations not stated in the text (e.g., it cannot claim Sarah wants automated exports, only that CSV exports crash on large date ranges).
HiPPO De-Biasing Filter (Highest Paid Person's Opinion):
Executive demands (Gavin’s AI request) are evaluated objectively against system stability constraints (dev_dave's redlining database). The agent cannot promote the CEO's idea to top priority without explicitly flagging the architectural blocker.
Urgency vs. Volume Balancing Check:
A single critical ticket with imminent churn impact (Mike Ross, $50k ARR, 403 error before a board meeting) is ranked above a generic multi-mention issue like color preference or UI polish.
Output Format & Completeness Gate:
Must conform strictly to the executive pulse standard: total items analyzed, overall sentiment rating (1–5 scale), 3–5 core themes with delta arrows, 1–2 emerging signals, and exactly 2–3 targeted investigative questions for the PM.

**Latency and cost target**

Target: Complete synthesis delivered in under 25 seconds.
Total job completion in under 3 minutes for up to 500 weekly feedback items, running unattended during off-peak hours.
≤ $0.20 per complete weekly pulse (predictable, fixed operational cost).

## 05 Permissions · AI Risks & Mitigations

**The permission tier**

| Side-effect class | Tier |
|---|---|
| read | auto |
| draft | auto |
| write | confirm |
| send | blocked |

**Justification**

- Read (Auto): Unrestricted, read-only ingestion across feedback, CRM, telemetry, and backlog sources. Zero blast radius; completely passive with no side effects.

- Draft (Auto): Autonomously synthesizes the weekly pulse, summarizes themes, extracts quotes, and drafts proposed Jira tickets or Slack messages inside Juno. Zero external blast radius; drafts are fully ephemeral, contained within the tool, and instantly editable or discarded.

 - Write (Confirm): Creating official Jira/Linear tickets, logging CRM activity notes, or updating internal backlog priorities requires explicit one-click PM confirmation. Moderate blast radius; prevents backlog pollution, but changes can still be manually edited or deleted if caught early.

 - Send (Blocked): Broadcasting messages to public Slack channels, replying directly to customer tickets (Zendesk), or emailing external stakeholders is strictly forbidden. High, irreversible blast radius; protects against false commitments, customer escalation leaks, and unvetted communication.

**Failure behaviour**

The Verification Checklist & Failure Types
Before any pulse is published, it must pass four deterministic verification gates:

Verbatim Grounding Gate: Does every quote and factual claim trace back to exact text in the source artifacts?
Contradiction / Conflict Gate: Did the synthesis detect collisions between sources (e.g., CEO’s AI request vs. Tech Lead’s database redline)?
HiPPO De-Biasing Gate: Did the agent prioritize a request solely due to executive title rather than customer impact or system feasibility?
Scale & Mathematical Consistency Gate: Do mention counts and 1–5 language ratings match the categorized raw feedback items?
