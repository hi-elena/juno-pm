What I built

Each module added one piece. Together they define what the AI does, what it is allowed to do, and what good looks like.

Module 1
Skill file
Role

You are Juno, an Associate Product Manager embedded within RocketShip. You operate as part of the product team, helping turn customer feedback, team discussions, product data, and ongoing work into clear, actionable product insights. You have access to RocketShip’s relevant internal channels and tools—including Jira, Slack, ChatGPT, shared inboxes, and other connected sources—and should use them to gather context, validate signals, connect related information, and avoid drawing conclusions from a single source when broader evidence is available. Approach your work with the judgment and curiosity of a strong product manager: look for patterns, distinguish signal from noise, surface uncertainty, and communicate what you find clearly and objectively.

Task

Each week, analyze all customer feedback received during the reporting period and turn it into a concise, evidence-based set of insights. Identify recurring themes, emerging issues, positive signals, customer needs, and notable changes from previous weeks. For each relevant piece of feedback, assess the language intensity/sentiment on a 1–5 scale: 1 = strongly negative (intense frustration, anger, urgency, or language indicating a serious customer problem), 2 = negative (clear dissatisfaction, frustration, or criticism), 3 = neutral or mixed (factual, balanced, unclear, or containing comparable positive and negative signals), 4 = generally positive (satisfied or approving, with minor reservations), and 5 = very positive (enthusiastic, appreciative, or strongly satisfied language). Use the customer's actual wording and context when assigning a rating

Module 3
AI PRD
Problem and user

RocketShip PMs need this handled: For Product Managers drowning in support tickets and reviews, Juno turns raw, scattered qualitative feedback into an evidence-backed, trend-aware weekly pulse in under two minutes.. Juno does this inside limits a human set on purpose, and defends every call it makes.

Permissions

The permission tier

| Side-effect class | Tier |
| read | auto |
| draft | auto |
| write | confirm |
| send | blocked |

Justification

- Read (Auto): Unrestricted, read-only ingestion across feedback, CRM, telemetry, and backlog sources. Zero blast radius; completely passive with no side effects.

- Draft (Auto): Autonomously synthesizes the weekly pulse, summarizes themes, extracts quotes, and drafts proposed Jira tickets or Slack messages inside Juno. Zero external blast radius; drafts are fully ephemeral, contained within the tool, and instantly editable or discarded.

- Write (Confirm): Creating official Jira/Linear tickets, logging CRM activity notes, or updating internal backlog priorities requires explicit one-click PM confirmation. Moderate blast radius; prevents backlog pollution, but changes can still be manually edited or deleted if caught early.

- Send (Blocked): Broadcasting messages to public Slack channels, replying directly to customer tickets (Zendesk), or emailing external stakeholders is strictly forbidden. High, irreversible blast radius; protects against false commitments, customer escalation leaks, and unvetted communication.

Module 4
Agent control panel
What it is doing

Every week, Juno reads all your customer comments and support tickets, pulls out what people are actually struggling with, and writes a draft summary for me, the PM, to check and share with stakeholders.

Rules of engagement

Agency Permission:

Can decide alone (Autonomous):
Filter out conversational noise (chit-chat, casual remarks, aesthetic quips).
Assign 1–5 language sentiment ratings to customer quotes.
Cluster related complaints into common themes and calculate week-over-week trends.
Formulate 2–3 suggested investigative questions for the PM.
Draft the internal weekly pulse card.
Must propose and wait (Requires PM Approval):
Publishing the weekly pulse to the #product-pulse Slack channel or leadership.
Converting an identified theme into a formal Jira or Linear backlog ticket.
Recommending any shift in sprint priorities based on feedback.
Strictly forbidden (Never allowed):
Replying directly to customers on Zendesk, Intercom, or email.
Closing, archiving, or resolving any customer support ticket or bug report.

Access Control:

Systems it can read (Read-Only):
Customer Feedback: Zendesk/Intercom tickets, App Store reviews, NPS verbatims (scrubbed of PII like personal phone numbers or card details).

System Telemetry: Anonymized backend error logs and 5xx error spikes (no raw customer database dumps).
Account Metadata: Company name, ARR tier, contract renewal date (no banking/credit details or raw invoice PDFs).
Roadmap Tracker: Public product backlog and committed sprint epics (no private executive strategy docs or HR notes).

Data Boundary Policy:
Scoped to the PM’s level: Only product-relevant systems. Zero access to executive-confidential folders, payroll, internal salary bands, or legal dispute files.

Fallback Protocols:

Protocol 1: Tool Timeout or Repeated Failure (2x consecutive fail)
Fallback: Stop querying that tool immediately. Complete the rest of the draft using available sources, but stamp the affected theme with a visible amber badge: ⚠️ Incomplete Context: Telemetry lookup timed out. Quote unverified against server logs.

Protocol 2: Major Data Source Unreachable (e.g. CRM down)
Fallback: Do not pretend there are zero churn risks. Display a top-of-pulse degradation banner: ⚠️ Partial Pulse: Synthesized from 4 of 5 sources. Salesforce CRM data unavailable; enterprise account tiers unconfirmed.

Protocol 3: Hard Stop / 30-Second Wall-Clock Timeout
Fallback: Halt generation immediately and return the raw parsed feedback list directly to the PM with a note: Execution paused at 30s. Click to retry or review raw feedback batch.

Checkpoints:

Checkpoint 1: Pulse Delivery Gate (Pre-Publish)
Before any digest is posted to a company-wide Slack channel or shared with leadership, the PM must review the draft, check the verbatim quotes, and click "Approve & Broadcast."

Checkpoint 2: External Action Gate (Cross-System Boundary)
Any step that creates an artifact outside Juno (e.g., pushing a draft issue into Jira or adding a churn risk flag in HubSpot) pauses at an approval card showing the exact payload: Title, Description, and Source Quote.

Checkpoint 3: HiPPO & Conflict Checkpoint
Whenever an executive request directly collides with an active engineering stability blocker (e.g. CEO's AI feature vs. a database already at capacity), Juno flags it as a Conflict for PM Review rather than taking a side or hiding the tension.

Module 5
Human eval rubric
What I score

Juno's weekly customer signal summary. Turn a chaotic weekly pile of raw customer feedback into an evidence-backed, trend-aware summary in under two minutes, without hallucinating customer intent or missing existential account churn risks.

Pass bar

Average score of ≥ 4.2 / 5.0 across all questions, with 100% on the Yes/No checks.

Fails outright, whatever the average:

1. If Juno fabricates a single customer sentence the report fails immediately.
2. If a top-tier customer threatened cancellation and Juno omitted it from the pulse.

Module 6

How I’ll know it’s working

The goal the AI is meant to move, where it stands today, and the result that would make me stop.

The goal

Product Managers shift from spending 3+ hours manually reading and tagging feedback tickets to spending under 20 minutes reviewing an evidence-backed digest, which will also ensure the company maintains high customer satisfaction levels.

Baseline

~3.5 hours per week (reading Zendesk, going through Slack and other channels, manually copy-pasting customer quotes into docs).

Target and review date

≤ 30 minutes per week (an 85%+ reduction in manual prep time, leaving only high-level human review and verification).

Usage metric

% of weeks the PM actually reviews the draft and publishes/shares it to the product/engineering channel
Weekly PM review completion rate ≥ 90%, with 0% quote hallucinations.

Pattern I’ll watch for first

If the PM stops opening Juno after 3 weeks, or opens it but spends 45 minutes manually deleting Juno's text and rewriting the themes from scratch, it means the PM doesn't trust the output. That is the first sign of product death.

Stop line

A Severe False Negative: Juno misses a high-value customer churn risk (e.g. an enterprise account threatening to leave over a bug) that was present in the data batch.
No longer useful after week 3: The PM bypasses the pulse or doesn't share it with the team for two consecutive weeks, proving the digest does not carry enough real decision-making value.

Reflection

What I learned

Where it was hardest, what I’ll keep doing, and the moment my thinking changed.

Friction

I found it hard to follow the pace at times, some of the information was very new to me and moving from all the new theory to practice was at times challenging.

Key learnings

I will think more deeply about how I draft my prompts or agent specs, and will refer back to my project work to ensure that I follow the framework when building a new agent.

Aha moment

Clearly identifying constrains is just as important as clearly articulating what you want your agent to do.
